# The Dog That Didn't Bark 闕文

**peira code:** `LACUNA` · **Modern — analytic and scientific method** · **Catalogued** — a reading, owns no gate

**Failure mode:** a distillation reports only what the source contains, so it is structurally blind to what a competent treatment of the topic would contain but this one omits — the missing counter-argument, dataset, caveat, or party

**What it does:** before a source is accepted as complete, enumerate what its topic demands and mark each item the source never addresses; the gap is a finding, not a silence

**Worked example:** Holmes solves it by the dog that did NOT bark: the absence of the expected reaction is the evidence. 孔子 keeps the counterpart — 「吾猶及史之闕文也」, the honest historian leaves a blank where knowledge is missing rather than filling it. A summary that silently omits what it never saw does the opposite, and reads as complete.

**In the tradition:** Two sources meet here. 孔子, in the Analects (15.26): 「吾猶及史之闕文也」— he could still recall when a scribe left a blank (闕文) rather than fill a gap he could not vouch for. And Conan Doyle's "Silver Blaze" (1892), where Holmes cracks the case by the dog that did not bark: the absence of the expected is the evidence. A distillation sees neither unbidden — it reports what is present, so an omission has to be hunted.
