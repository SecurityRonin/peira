# Name the Warrant

<!-- Generated from peira_lens::CATALOG by cli/examples/gen_site.rs. Edit the lens in lens/src/lib.rs and regenerate. -->

**peira code:** `TOULMIN` · **Modern — analytic and scientific method** · **Enforced** — owns a gate

**Failure mode:** the unstated warrant: grounds and claim are given, the rule connecting them never is

**What it does:** `warrant` is a required field, not an optional one

**Enforced gates:** `PEIR-WARRANT-MISSING`

**Worked example:** Toulmin's own case: from "Harry was born in Bermuda" to "Harry is a British subject" the step rides on an unwritten warrant — the law that a Bermudan is a British subject. Written down, a warrant can be checked or shown false; left unwritten, it just works.

**In the tradition:** Stephen Toulmin, in The Uses of Argument (1958), held that real reasoning is not the syllogism but a structure of claim, grounds, and — crucially — a warrant: the inference-licence saying why the grounds bear on the claim, itself resting on backing and softened by qualifiers and rebuttals. His own case runs from "Harry was born in Bermuda" to "Harry is a British subject" via the warrant of Bermudan law. The lens makes the warrant a required field: the step from grounds to claim must be written, not assumed.
